What is an eight-thousander?

Fourteen mountains on Earth rise above 8,000 metres — the altitude at which, near the summit, the air holds roughly a third of the oxygen available at sea level. Ten of them stand in the Himalaya; four in the Karakoram. Eight are wholly or partly in Nepal, the rest in Pakistan, China and, at one shared border, India.

The first, Annapurna, was climbed in 1950. The last, Shishapangma, in 1964 — delayed only by politics. Everything below is an atlas of those fourteen summits.
